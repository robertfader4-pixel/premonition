Robert Fader · Premonition

Chapter 6: Enemies

✦

Kenneth and Martin arrived at the savages’ camp on a motorcycle. They slowly approached the edge of the camp, turned off the engine, raised a white flag, and walked toward the people waiting for them. As they drew nearer, the figures of their opponents became clearer. They were all strongly built, with long tangled hair and wild beards, dressed in scraps of old clothing. Medical masks covered their mouths and noses. Their eyes, barely visible beneath heavy brows, watched with suspicion and distrust.

The camp stood on a rise surrounded by dense forest. Around its perimeter, campfires had been placed like distant trembling lights, reflected in the eyes of the sentries.

In the center of the camp rose a large wooden structure decorated with various trophies and symbols of power. On its walls one could see brightly colored shields bearing the emblem of the famous football club Boca Juniors, as well as posters of their charismatic politician with his distinctive long hair and sideburns. There were also posters showing a snake on a yellow background with the slogan “Don’t tread on me.”

The air was filled with the smells of fernet and choripán, the smoked-sausage sandwich used in their rituals. As their flag, they proudly flew the dollar sign, which embodied their ideals of freedom and independence.

One of the savages, clearly the chief judging by his imposing posture and the heavy necklace of bones around his neck, stepped forward. He was half a head taller than the others, with broad shoulders and a threatening gaze. His movements were confident and measured, as if he were accustomed to leading his people under any circumstances.

Kenneth and Martin both held signs of peaceful intent — placards with messages written in a language both sides could understand. They approached the chief cautiously, trying not to show fear. Around them stood several followers, ready to protect their leaders if necessary. The chief of the savages studied the visitors closely. He took a step forward, his heavy boots thudding against the hard earth.

— We are not used to receiving uninvited guests, — he said in a deep, rough voice that echoed through the camp. — What are you doing here?

Kenneth, maintaining his composure, answered:

— We have come in search of peace and cooperation. We need to discuss the terms of peaceful coexistence between our peoples.

Martin added:

— We are prepared to offer help and resources that may be useful to your camp. In return, we ask only for respect and safe passage through your territory.

The chief exchanged glances with the other leaders of the camp. The negotiations continued for a long time and included many discussions, proposals, and objections. The savages remained wary, but they also showed interest in what Kenneth and Martin had to offer. They spoke of trade, the exchange of knowledge, and possible cooperation in protecting the camp from outside threats.

Yet despite every effort, the negotiations began to stall. Disagreements over the distribution of resources, mistrust of each other’s intentions, and cultural differences became obstacles too great to overcome. With every passing hour, tension in the camp increased, and the atmosphere grew hotter. At one point, one of the savages sharply rejected a proposal, provoking a storm of anger among the others. Kenneth and Martin immediately understood that further negotiations were doomed. They decided to leave the camp and return to the fortress to reconsider their strategy. After respectfully saying farewell to the savage leaders, they quickly mounted the motorcycle and headed back.

Both men were lost in thought. They understood that the failure of the talks might lead to inevitable conflict. When they returned to the fortress, the other members of the team were already waiting for them. Kenneth and Martin hurried to the main hall, where Robert received them.

— Robert, — Kenneth began, his voice firm, — we were unable to reach an agreement with the savages. The negotiations failed.

Martin added:

— They are not inclined toward cooperation. Continuing to try may only make the situation worse. We have no choice but to prepare for a fight.

Robert listened to them carefully, his face grave. Then he went up to the fortress’s upper observation platform. He stood there, looking out over the landscape spread before him. Harsh mountains, shifting from green to gray, sheltered his people; below, beyond the irrigation ditch, lay settlements that had once been full of life but now stood abandoned, soaked in recent blood and suffering. The team gathered around him was not large, yet their determination could be felt in the very air — every face reflected courage. At the same time, many questions circled in Robert’s mind. Recent failures had exposed vulnerabilities that had to be corrected before they could continue their struggle.

After standing still for a moment, Robert looked over all his companions. Each of them was ready to support him, but right now what they lacked most was information. He knew that in order to reach their goal, they had to understand the nature of their enemy. With that thought in mind, he decided to turn to Luis, the one person who might help uncover the riddles hidden behind the hostile eyes of the savages.

Luis, who had grown up among the savages before the catastrophe, became an indispensable source of information for Robert. Although his relationship with that people was complicated, his memory still held recollections of beautiful moments, of a culture, of principles and morals that had governed the lives of the savages. He knew their peculiarities, and he also knew their capacity for betrayal. If anyone could help them extinguish the conflict, it was Luis.

— Luis, — Robert said, his voice confident and insistent, — you know these savages better than anyone. What can you tell me about them? I feel we cannot simply fight without understanding who we are dealing with.

Luis sighed, as if the last memories of his youth among the savages had returned to him. A longing for that time appeared on his face.

— They are not merely enemies, — he began. — There is much more in their lives than primitive hostility. Yes, they are cruel and they do not trust outsiders, but they have a culture that differs sharply from ours. They have their own moral codes, even if those codes may seem savage to us.

Sitting on one of the stones, Luis continued to speak of their traditions, rituals, and their understanding of honor and dignity. Yet he emphasized that their mistrust of outsiders was enormous — especially when they felt threatened. There could be no question of allowing anyone, even with good intentions, to trespass on their land without consequences.

— If you truly want to try again, — Luis said, his expression serious, — you must understand that they will notice all our weaknesses. They will be on guard the moment they suspect we are hiding something. If they see us as a threat, or even glimpse the slightest weakness, they may attack.

Luis’s words made Robert think. He understood that during the previous encounter they had failed to make the right impression on the savages. Perhaps that was a mistake they could not afford to repeat. Deciding that they had to make one more attempt, he turned to Luis.

— You must help me reach them, — Robert insisted. — I am almost certain I can persuade them to join our cause, if only we can make them understand our intentions.

Luis nodded, though worry remained on his face. He knew that understanding had to be achieved not only through words, but also through deeds. They mounted the motorcycle; Luis took the white flag in his hands as a sign of peaceful intentions. Robert started the engine, and they set out toward the savages’ camp.

On the way there, Luis’s mood troubled him, though he tried to drive away thoughts of possible failure. Yet as they approached the camp, his anxiety grew. He saw their motorcycle quickly surrounded by dissatisfied, whispering people who looked like guardians of their territory. Robert knew that if this conversation failed, they might not leave the place alive.

Silence fell over the camp when they arrived. People who had been coming and going about their business suddenly stopped and turned their eyes toward the newcomers. Robert felt that the moment had come. Slowly, he climbed onto a small rise so that everyone present could hear him.

— Comrades! — he said loudly enough for his voice to carry through the entire camp. — I am here to create a new world. A new world in which we will work together and stand against the threats before us. I invite you to help us build a second defensive line around our fortress, which we call Our Utopia.

But as soon as he spoke those words, Robert saw the gaze of the chief savage, Juan, fill with displeasure. There was genuine aggression in it, as if he were preparing to enter open conflict with Robert at any moment. Juan belonged to that kind of person who fiercely defended his traditions and could not allow his lands to become the object of someone else’s ambitions.

— Do you really believe we are your servants? — Juan said with irony in his voice, his face twisting into a sneer, while the other savages stared grimly, waiting for his answer. Some laughed spitefully at the situation.

Robert felt the atmosphere growing more tense, but he did not intend to retreat. He had to communicate his idea.

— We are working for the common good, — he continued. — Let us unite and create a new space for all of us, a place where we can live in peace and calm without fearing our enemies. Together we can become stronger.

But his words enraged Juan, who burst into loud laughter. It was not only hostile laughter, but degrading laughter, revealing all his contempt for Robert.

— You want us to join this socialist project and become part of your “utopia”? Are you joking? — he said. His words drew approving shouts from the savages. The laughter became deafening, and Robert understood that they were no longer in a position to change the situation.

Luis, standing close to the savages, felt their patience run out. He saw the tension around them swelling, ready to turn into open confrontation. Robert and Luis realized that their efforts had been futile. Dissatisfied and disappointed, they turned and left the camp, leaving behind a roar of angry voices. As they climbed back onto the motorcycle, Juan shouted after them:

— Hey, traitor! You will pay for everything!

So they recognized me after all. Good thing we are already leaving, Luis thought.

On the road back to the fortress, Robert was filled with anger. He knew that the collapse of negotiations and the failure to establish peaceful coexistence could end in disaster. Lost in thought, he hardly noticed the gloomy landscape around him, for his head was occupied by a single idea: they could not afford another absurd failure.

— Why did they call Our Utopia a socialist project? — Robert asked Luis.

— That’s what they call everything. But what do we do now? — Luis asked, his voice frightened and full of doubt.

— We cannot simply sit and wait, — Robert said, frowning, his eyes burning with an impatient fire. — We must prepare our weapons. If they want to fight, we will show them what we are made of.

Luis, feeling anxiety and uncertainty, could not tell whether this was the right thing to do. His fears covered him like a storm, and he did not know what to expect next.

— Do you really think this is the only way? — he asked cautiously.

Robert looked at him angrily.

— In times like these, — he answered firmly, — sometimes the only way to protect your people is to be ready for battle. We must stand strong in order to defend our fortress.

Luis understood that standing on the edge of conflict was not what he desired, but he had no choice except to agree with Robert. That same night, they began preparing for the inevitable, shaping a strategy and distributing tasks among their people. Each new day became another leap in their preparation for a battle that burned more and more intensely in their minds and hearts.

— You know, Luis, — Robert began, — I have always considered myself a humanist. My ideology and philosophy are founded on respect for human life and dignity. That is why, before making decisions like these, it is important to be sure we truly have no other choice.

Luis looked into his friend’s eyes and asked:

— Aren’t you afraid?

Robert gave a faint smile.

— I survived a catastrophe on a nuclear scale. There is nothing left for me to fear. We must act, and if that means fighting, then I am ready.

The next morning, as the sounds of ordinary life drifted through the fortress, Robert felt the need to gather everyone once more. He stationed people on defense and ordered them to remain alert. They knew that the savages would not wait before responding to an attack. Each of them had to be ready for the conflict to become reality, not merely a threat.

When the team had assembled, Robert gave a short speech.

— We may be unhappy with what happened, but that does not mean we should lose our resolve. We will defend our fortress, and no one will take our home from us. We must stand together as one team, because only in unity will we find strength.

He knew that the morale of everyone present depended on his words. Every person in the fortress could be one of those who contributed to the future of this place.

They had not only to discuss their actions, but to think them through and find the proper directions. They began developing strategies to protect their borders before open conflict was declared. Too much was at stake, and Robert understood that his decision must not narrow their possibilities.

Over the next several days, tension continued to rise. Robert and Luis decided that they needed to act actively, and their strategic intentions became reality. Gradually, supporters began to gather around them. People believed in their goals and were ready to support them in this effort.

Meanwhile, in the savages’ camp, no one was idle either. Juan addressed his people:

— We cannot be mere targets for the ambitions of strangers, — he shouted, stirring unity among them. Each of his words gave them courage and backbone. — We will show these utopians that our ideals of freedom are stronger than theirs. Let us crush their filthy fortress.

Two worlds were preparing to collide. With each passing day, they opposed each other not only in war, but in an ideological conflict.

The savages gathered near the fortress and prepared for the assault.

Battle was inevitable, but Robert felt ready for it. He believed their fortress would become a place of protection and a safe refuge. He did not abandon hope for compromise, but he was ready to finish this struggle if peace proved impossible.

Gathering their strength, Robert and his team began the final preparations. Each of them understood that what was at stake was not only their lives, but the future of their people. They trained, refined their skills, and developed strategies so they would be ready for any turn of events.

The night before the battle was full of tension. Each of them knew that morning would bring not only trials, but also the possibility of changing the course of history. Robert gathered his people and delivered an inspiring speech. He reminded them that they were fighting not only for themselves, but for their loved ones, and for the future they wanted to build.

— We are not simply defending our fortress, — he said. — We are defending our dreams, our families, and our culture. We must be united and steadfast as never before. Together we can overcome any obstacle. Remember: we are the only humanists left on earth.

With those words, they dispersed to their positions, preparing for the battle ahead. Each of them knew that this would not be merely a fight, but a struggle for survival, for the right to exist in this cruel world.

When dawn colored the sky in brilliant tones, Robert and his team took their positions. They were ready to face an enemy they knew would not surrender without a fight. Tension hung in the air, and every one of them felt that this battle would determine their fate.
