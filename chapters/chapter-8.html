Robert Fader · Premonition

Chapter 8: Humanism

✦

Two months had passed since the savages had tried to attack Our Utopia and suffered a crushing defeat. Now life had become peaceful. Sasha thought constantly about resources and did everything she could to use them wisely.

The inhabitants of the great fortress began to enjoy their new life. Children played without fear. Even a sport that, before the global catastrophe, had stirred so many emotions was restored: football.

Luis did not like seeing the children play it. It reminded him of his homeland, where the savages had adored the game and had been ready to unleash conflict because of it.

Robert stopped Luis and advised him to relax. There were worse things in life, he reminded him, and the children should be allowed to enjoy themselves. It was summer now, and soon they would have to go to school.

Meanwhile, some of the wounded savages who had accepted Robert’s offer during the attack and become part of Our Utopia began working actively inside the fortress. They understood that violence and aggression led nowhere good, and now they sought a peaceful life. Their skills and experience proved useful in rebuilding and developing the fortress.

The savages, once considered enemies, became part of the community. They helped construct new buildings, tend the gardens, and teach children different crafts. Gradually, trust began to grow between the inhabitants of the fortress and their new allies. Sasha watched this process and felt her heart fill with hope.

She understood that humanism and mutual understanding were the key to creating a new world. Every day they worked together, overcoming the prejudices and fears that had once divided them. Now they were becoming a single whole, striving toward a common good.

Robert watched proudly as the fortress grew and developed. He was glad to see that they had overcome obstacles on the road toward peace and understanding. He knew that many trials still lay ahead, but for the moment they had the chance to create the future they had all dreamed of for so long.

Robert had already appointed a small group of teachers. Sasha would teach mathematics; Kenneth would teach survival skills; Martin would teach martial arts and physical training.

Luis interrupted Robert and asked permission to read from his Manifesto of Humanism, a manifesto he intended to share with other people. Robert found the idea interesting and asked Luis to read a passage aloud. Luis began:

1. Humanism as a Fundamental Pillar. Humanism, born in the Renaissance, became a beacon in the search for human dignity, creativity, and potential. We reject both the rigid theology of the Middle Ages and a purely mechanistic view of humanity. Instead, we embrace an anthropocentric perspective that recognizes the individual’s potential for full realization. This rebirth of humanism must adapt itself to the complex challenges of the twenty-first century, ensuring that reason, ethics, and respect for diversity become the foundation of our society.

2. Disillusionment and Existential Crisis. We recognize that humanity has undergone a critical estrangement from humanist ideals. Wars, economic crises, and the growing dehumanization of the individual have produced a deep disillusionment. This crisis manifests not only in politics, where selfish interests prevail over the common good, but also in society, where alienation increases our collective suffering. We stand at a decisive moment: the choice between the barbarism of individualism and the rebirth of true humanism.

“I like this very much,” Robert said. “I think it expresses exactly what we want.”

At that moment Kenneth’s shout rang out.

“Someone is coming!”

A group of very well-dressed people was approaching the fortress. Kenneth went out to meet them.

“Who are you?”

A rather attractive blonde woman removed her veil and said her name was Oksana. She was a singer, and she wanted to give a concert.

Oksana was a captivating figure, radiating elegance and refinement. Her black dress, decorated with delicate golden details, emphasized her slender, feminine figure. The shine of her necklace and earrings perfectly complemented her blue eyes, which seemed to glow with their own light. Although she was not tall, her presence was striking, and her golden hair fell in soft waves that drew everyone’s attention. Oksana was not merely attractive; she radiated confidence, which made her even more enchanting.

The woman beside her, with dark chestnut hair, offered an intriguing contrast. She was a little taller and slimmer than Oksana, and her posture was equally elegant. Her long hair, arranged in a refined bun, emphasized the delicate lines of her face. Her blue eyes, like Oksana’s, possessed a depth that hinted at a hidden story. Although her appearance was more restrained, her presence was strong and assured, suggesting that she was no ordinary companion.

Finally, the guard who accompanied both women added an element of mystery. His stance was firm and watchful, yet it was not clear whom he was truly protecting — Oksana, the singer, or the dark-haired woman. His presence suggested that there was more to the situation than first appeared, and that the arrival of these women at the fortress might carry deeper consequences.

Robert thought it might be a trap. Their expensive clothing struck him as deeply suspicious. Oksana was accompanied by two bodyguards and another woman carrying an instrument. It looked like a cello. Robert decided to question them.

He invited them into a special room, well furnished and carefully decorated, though its purpose now was interrogation.

“What do you want? What did you do before the Great Catastrophe, and what do you hope to achieve in Our Utopia?”

Robert sat at the long table in that special room, where an atmosphere of gravity and caution prevailed. He studied the strangers, trying to detect any sign of deception or hidden intent.

Oksana, dressed in an elegant gown, with a faint smile on her lips and confidence in her gaze, took the central place. Her two bodyguards stood at her sides as if to protect her from any threat, while the woman with the cello settled quietly nearby.

“Welcome to Our Utopia,” Robert began, choosing his words carefully. “My name is Robert, and I am here to ask you several questions. We need to understand your true intentions.”

Oksana nodded, showing that she was ready to speak.

“What do you want from us?” Robert asked, watching her reaction closely.

“I come in peace,” Oksana replied, her voice musical. “I am a singer, and my dream is to bring people joy through music. I have heard about your fortress and how art and culture have begun to revive here. I would like to perform a concert for you and your people.”

Robert frowned and decided to press further.

“How did you end up in this place?” he continued, never taking his eyes from hers. “You appear to be dressed in expensive clothes. That is not the usual appearance of people living through difficult times.”

Oksana answered confidently.

“These clothes were given to me in the City of Obituaries. It is a place where many people prepared themselves for catastrophe. It became a refuge for multimillionaires who hid underground. I performed there, and that gave me the opportunity to receive such things. People are willing to pay for live music even in the hardest times.”

Robert shook his head, not fully convinced that she was telling the truth.

“And what were you doing in this Necropolis?” he asked, sensing that much more might be hidden behind her words.

“I performed at events organized by wealthy people,” Oksana answered, gently touching the cello that stood beside her. “It was a way for them to forget their anxieties and enjoy the moment. I dreamed of becoming known, and that opened certain doors for me.”

Seeing that her answers were coming too easily, Robert decided to put her under more pressure.

“Why did you not stay in the place where you had been given a comfortable life? Why risk coming here, where everything is being built from nothing?”

Oksana looked directly into his eyes and replied without losing confidence.

“Because I believe in the power of music, and I believe I can help people come together. In Our Utopia I see the possibility of being part of something greater. I want to inspire you and your people toward a new beginning — one that is bright and beautiful.”

Robert continued to wonder whether she might be a trap disguised as an ally.

“Then tell me how you plan to do that. How do you intend to influence the people who live here?”

Oksana drew a deep breath and paused for a moment.

“Music is a language everyone understands. I want to bring art into your lives, awaken good emotions in people, and remind them what it means to be human.”

Beside Oksana stood the mysterious girl with blue eyes and dark hair. She watched Robert’s interrogation with a smile.

“I offer you not only a concert,” Oksana continued, “but cooperation with local musicians, so that together we may revive a culture buried by the dark times.”

Robert did not answer at once. Many thoughts collided in his mind, and he knew this was not a decision to rush. He looked at his companions and finally spoke.

“Very well. We will allow you to perform. But before any of that begins, we will be watching you.”

Relief flashed involuntarily in Oksana’s eyes, but Robert remained cautious. Too much was at stake, and trust had to be given carefully.

Oksana explained again that she had received her clothing in the City of Obituaries. Robert did not understand the word.

“Who are the obituaries?”

Oksana answered that she had been fortunate enough to perform in an underground city far away, where many multimillionaires had purchased homes in case of apocalypse. They lived there and waited for the moment when they could emerge.

At that moment Martin entered and was struck by Oksana’s enchanting beauty.

After Oksana, Martin, and her guards left the room, the atmosphere changed. Tension remained in the air, but with it came the expectation of something unusual. Robert, still unsettled by the mysterious blue-eyed woman, raised his eyebrows and asked:

“What is your name?”

“Natalia,” she answered.

Natalia wore a shimmering dress whose sparkle and elegant cut immediately drew the eye. The dress embraced her figure, emphasizing her femininity and grace. Although it was summer outside, she wore a light jacket that added a stylish, slightly mysterious note to her appearance. Made of thin material, the jacket harmonized with the dress and created an image both bright and refined.

At that moment Robert’s heart began to beat faster, and he did not understand why. He told Natalia that she had a very beautiful name. She smiled playfully and asked:

“Why did you not say the same to Oksana?”

“Because you are the one who awakened my admiration,” Robert replied.

He could hardly believe it. Was he flirting with another woman? Yes, with a woman who bore the same name as his wife — and yet he was doing it. His heart beat faster and faster, and he felt excitement and confusion grow inside his chest.

Then he asked about the cello, and Natalia answered:

“Yes, I play. For me, the cello is not simply an instrument. It is my soul. Music is a way of expressing emotions that are often too difficult to put into words.”

Robert stood and moved closer to Natalia, who seemed lost in her own thoughts. He noticed how carefully her hands wandered over the strings, as though they themselves were searching for the right melodic path.

“Why did you choose this path? Sadly, music is not a priority in our world now,” he said, genuinely interested in her answer.

Natalia sighed, as if preparing to reveal something important.

“I have always believed that in the darkest times, art and music can become the light at the end of the tunnel. I have seen how music can inspire people, give hope, and bring them together. When I play, I feel I can do something meaningful, even if it is only one minute of peace in a stormy world.”

Then she began to play. The melody that came from the cello filled the room with astonishing depth and humanity.

Lost in the music, Robert felt his heart beat in harmony with each note. This was more than music. It was the expression of a soul filled with joy, sorrow, and hope. As Natalia played, Robert wondered how art could become a transforming force. Could the concert Oksana had proposed truly become the spark that would kindle a fire in their community? Perhaps he was too cautious, too enclosed within his own fears and distrust.

At last Natalia finished playing. A magnificent silence filled the room, and Robert felt that the moment had been priceless.

“That was beautiful,” he said with sincere admiration. “I am certain you possess a gift that can change people. But what are your intentions? Why is this concert important to you?”

Natalia smiled.

“I want to give joy back to people. Your fortress has been a place where many have known only emptiness and fear. Music can help restore what has been lost and create new bonds. I dream that when you and your people hear my notes, you will remember your feelings and what it means to be together.”

Robert considered her words. The trials his people had faced had indeed been severe, and perhaps art could become a form of healing.

“I cannot promise anything immediately,” Robert said, “but I am willing to listen to your ideas. Perhaps we can begin with you working with our local musicians.”

Natalia nodded, and Robert felt that this meeting might be the beginning of something greater. Still, his caution remained. Trust had yet to be earned.

Robert asked Natalia one final question.

“Why do you and Oksana work together? Are you part of the same musical group?”

“I met Oksana in Necrology. When I saw how brilliantly she performed at a concert, I immediately invited her to join the tour I am organizing.”
