Robert Fader · Premonition

Chapter 3: Ghosts

✦

Several months had passed since they had found shelter in the abandoned fortress, and life in this new world had become a true ordeal. Robert, Alexey, and several new companions — among them skilled mechanics and hunters — decided to set out on a dangerous motorcycle expedition to obtain the materials they needed to strengthen their defensive positions. They knew they would not survive without reliable protection, not while they lived under the constant threat of bandits and the wild animals that roamed the surrounding lands. Armed with everything they needed, they rode out into the endless sands, a place full of danger and surprise, hoping that luck would remain on their side.

A dusty wind shook the invisible boundaries of the desert, creating mirages beneath a sun whose blinding rays cut through the air and left scorched scars across the endless dunes. Alexey, Robert, Zhenya, and Martin sped across the merciless sands on their motorcycles, searching for salvation. Their iron horses roared against the wind, leaving a wavering cloud of dust behind them.

Suddenly, a sinister shadow appeared on the horizon — armed men on motorcycles were closing in fast. Their engines roared like wild beasts, and the attackers’ faces were hidden behind masks and torn strips of cloth. Each of them wore battered leather jackets decorated with trophies from past battles: teeth, feathers, and metal spikes. Their eyes glowed with a thirst for violence, and in their hands they held sharp knives and clubs, ready to attack. The atmosphere tightened to its breaking point, and tension, like a crown of thorns, encircled everyone on the road.

— “Look,” Alexey said quietly, staring toward the approaching figures. “It seems we’re not alone.”

Seconds later, a horde of savages burst out from behind the rocks, their strained faces showing only aggression and the desire to seize what they wanted. Robert and his companions understood at once that they had ridden into an ambush.

Battle on Motorcycles

— “Get out of here!” Alexey shouted as the first collisions began.

Robert instantly shifted into combat mode. With one hand he controlled the motorcycle, and with the other he held his sword, ready to defend his companions. Dust rose from their wheels and mingled with the clash of metal and the roar of engines. The savages attacked with cunning and skill: sharp turns, sudden leaps from motorcycles, and swift assaults with knives and iron rods. Every movement demanded an immediate reaction.

Martin swung a baseball bat, deflecting one warrior’s strike, while Zhenya fired her pistol with precision. But there were too many enemies. Robert fought off several attacks at once, his sword whistling through the air as it turned back the enemy’s pressure. Amid the chaos, he saw Alexey take a bullet to the chest. Despite the wound, Alexey reacted quickly, saving Robert from a savage’s deadly blow. Just as the attacker was about to strike, Alexey rammed him with his motorcycle, leaping in and dragging Robert away from danger.

— “We have to retreat!” Robert ordered, his voice cutting through the roar of the desert and echoing across the endless dunes. He felt his heart pounding in his chest as adrenaline flooded every cell of his body. Their strength was running out, and it was obvious that further resistance would become hopeless. All of his companions — Alexey, Zhenya, and Martin — understood the gravity of the situation without hesitation. Their motorcycles howled like enraged beasts as they accelerated, trying to break free of the desert.

Whirlwinds of sand surrounded them, blurring their vision and making every movement dangerous. The savages pursuing them had no intention of giving up. The motorcycles gained speed aggressively, their engines humming in unison with the suffocating breath of the desert.

Robert understood that without a clear plan their chances of survival were shrinking fast. He quickly pulled a map from under his turtleneck, already sticking beneath his leather jacket in the burning heat. Azure route lines shimmered across it like guiding stars in the night sky. He pointed to the coordinates of a place he believed would be ideal for building a temporary stronghold — a narrow gorge sheltered from the wind and protected by natural barriers.

— “Martin, take Zhenya and Alexey there. I’ll stay behind and face the savages,” Robert said, his voice firm and full of determination. He knew this decision could prove decisive for him and for the entire team.

Martin, his closest comrade and most reliable mechanic, nodded, understanding the weight of the order. His eyes met Robert’s, reflecting a mixture of resolve and concern. At once, Martin turned his motorcycle, signaled the others, and sped across the sands, carrying Zhenya and Alexey toward the safe place. As his engine thundered, he felt the fierce wind pulling them into the distance, leaving danger behind.

Robert remained alone. His only weapon was his sword — heavy and deadly, held firmly in his hands. He felt the chill of the metal between his fingers, his skin trembling slightly from tension. The sun was already leaning toward the horizon, casting long shadows over the sand.

Then, far ahead, a figure appeared against the horizon — the tall silhouette of a man approaching Robert. It was Samuel, the chief savage, leader of the hostile group. His body was imposing, his muscles taut beneath leather armor decorated with spikes and runic symbols that proclaimed his status and power. Samuel’s dark, penetrating eyes shone with determination and a hunger for authority.

— “What do you want?” Robert asked without looking away. His voice was calm, but it carried an iron resolve to protect his team.

Samuel came closer, his steps confident and almost silent despite the sandstorm around him. He stopped at arm’s length, slowly swinging a long sword adorned with ancient runes that seemed to glimmer in the half-light of sunset.

— “Give us all the women in your group,” he said, his voice cold and threatening, like thunder under a clear sky.

Robert felt a cold line of sweat slide down his back, but he did not allow panic to take hold. He knew that negotiations with such an opponent were unlikely to succeed, yet staying calm was the only way to survive. His mind searched rapidly for possible choices, but his deepest conviction stood in defense of his companions.

— “I agree,” he answered, tightening his grip on the sword, “but only on one condition: we duel by the rules.”

Samuel smirked. His sword flashed in the light of the setting sun, and his eyes glittered with excitement and challenge.

— “Good. Then we’ll see whose skill is greater,” he replied, taking his fighting stance. The sword in his hand seemed alive, ready for destruction.

A fierce duel began. Samuel attacked first, his movements quick and sure, every stroke designed to inflict maximum damage. Each time the blade sliced through the air, Robert felt his breath quicken, but he remained focused. His body responded instinctively, guided by years of training and the need to survive.

Samuel did not leave Robert a single second to think. His attacks were aggressive and uncompromising. Robert dodged with precision, his movements controlled and graceful despite the pressure of the situation. Sand swirls pushed them toward the steep dunes, where a single mistake could cost a life. The battle was not only physical but psychological: each man tried to break the other’s will.

Samuel’s sword whistled through the air, cutting through the silence of the desert and leaving destruction in its wake. Robert, however, remained unyielding. He used every advantage he possessed — agility, strength, and strategy. At times he withdrew, luring Samuel into traps created by the rhythm of their combat. Every block and parry came from a careful reading of his opponent’s movements.

— “You will never defeat me,” Samuel growled, his voice trembling with rage and exhaustion as he unleashed a series of powerful blows that Robert could barely deflect.

Surrendering himself to the moment, Robert began to anticipate his opponent’s movements. He loosened his sword for a fraction of a second, letting Samuel miss, then struck back unexpectedly, forcing the savage to retreat a step. His muscles tightened, and each blow brought not only physical pain but also the emotional satisfaction of defending his friends.

Exhausted by endless attacks, they breathed harder and harder, and their movements began to slow. Robert noticed the slightest delay in Samuel’s motion — a sign of fatigue, or perhaps doubt. Taking advantage of that instant, he resolved to use everything he had. In one decisive moment, he made a swift turn and moved around his opponent from behind. Samuel, stunned by the sudden maneuver, tried to react, but it was too late. With one hand still controlling the motorcycle and the other delivering a powerful strike, Robert pierced the savage’s defense. His sword tore through Samuel’s protective layer and sank into skin and muscle. The leader’s upper body shuddered under the force of the blow before he collapsed onto the sand, defeated.

— “Who were you before the catastrophe?” Robert demanded, refusing to relax. His eyes continued to watch every movement of his enemy, ready for any unexpected turn. Barely breathing, Samuel raised his head. His eyes now expressed pain and the bitter recognition of defeat.

— “We fought for the Great Power,” he whispered, his voice trembling with exhaustion and resentment.

Robert looked over his enemy, and their eyes met. In that moment, a spark of understanding passed between them — two warriors who had fought for their causes and for survival, now standing on opposite sides of fate.

Without a moment’s hesitation, Robert raised his sword over his head and delivered a merciless killing blow. The blade sank deeper, and Samuel’s pain escaped him in a low groan. Blood quickly spread across the sand, forming a scarlet stain that marked the end of their confrontation. Seeing the execution of their leader and terrified by Robert’s skill, the remaining savages froze in shock and confusion. Some threw down their weapons. Others began to retreat, realizing that the force holding them together was gone.

As blood darkened the sand, the remaining pursuers disappeared, leaving Robert in the victorious silence of the desert. The wind died down, leaving only the soft rustle of grains of sand. Exhausted, Robert could barely breathe; his heart beat loudly in his chest. That morning seemed to him to have lasted an eternity.

He slowly lowered his sword. Weariness spread through his body, but an inner force pushed him onward. He looked around to make sure the enemies had truly withdrawn. The sun had begun to set, painting the horizon in warm shades of orange and red, creating the illusion of peace and calm.

— “That’s it,” he whispered to himself, feeling the rush of adrenaline gradually subside and give way to the harshness of reality. “Now I must keep my promise.”

Thinking of Zhenya, Alexey, and Martin’s safety, Robert rose to his feet. His muscles ached, and his wounds began to close slowly beneath the improvised netting he had quickly gathered from the materials at hand. He understood that their journey through the desert was only beginning, and that even more dangerous and unpredictable trials awaited them. But now, armed with renewed determination and the experience of battle, Robert was ready to face whatever fate had prepared for them.

The sun finally disappeared beyond the horizon, leaving behind the soft glow of the moon as it slowly rose to light the road. Robert took a deep breath and welcomed the cool night air, knowing that new challenges lay ahead — and also the hope of a brighter future for his friends and for himself.

Yet when he reached the place where he had left his companions, his heart clenched in horror. Alexey was lying on the ground, his face pale, his eyes no longer lit by life. Robert rushed to him, but it was too late. Alexey, who had fought bravely to the end, had become a victim of merciless fate.

Overcome by grief and resolve, Robert made a vow: in Alexey’s honor, he would raise a fortress that would become a secure refuge for everyone who had survived. In memory of his friend, he would name it Our Utopia — the place Alexey had once dreamed of, a safe home where people could live in peace and harmony. That name became a symbol of hope and of the longing for a better future, the future they had all been searching for so desperately in this cruel world.
