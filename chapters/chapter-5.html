Robert Fader · Premonition

Chapter 5: Premonition

✦

Robert and Kenneth gathered the bravest and most reliable residents of the fortress in the main hall. Among them was Sasha, a red-haired young woman with a piercing gaze.

Before the global catastrophe, Sasha had worked as an accountant, but her life changed when she accidentally overheard a conversation in the bunker where she had taken shelter during the nuclear winter. The discussion of creating Our Utopia — a new society founded on justice and unity — lit a spark of hope within her. Sasha realized that she wanted to become part of something greater than mere survival.

She left her former life behind and joined the project, bringing with her skills in organization and resource management that made her indispensable to the fortress’s team of defenders. Her ability to analyze a situation and find optimal solutions became a valuable asset in the face of the approaching threat.

Martin was a former soldier with impressive physical training and skill in martial arts, making him indispensable among the fortress defenders. He specialized in close combat and could make quick decisions in critical situations, though his impulsive character sometimes led him to reckless actions.

Martin had joined the project of Our Utopia almost by accident, when Alexey, one of the founders, asked him for help in searching for materials to build the fortress. At that moment, Martin felt that he could contribute to the creation of a new society, and from then on he became a devoted protector of his new family. His experience and decisiveness inspired others, and his desire to protect those dear to him made him an important member of the team.

Robert stepped onto the improvised platform, his face dark. He began to speak about the need to strengthen the defenses and about the importance of unity in the face of a possible threat. But at the very height of his speech, he suddenly felt the ground slipping out from beneath him. A sharp pain pierced his temples, and darkness clouded his vision.

Before his inner sight appeared Natalia. She was leaning over a wounded soldier, skillfully bandaging his injuries. Her hands moved confidently and precisely, bringing relief to the suffering man. But then the vision was shattered by a deafening explosion. Flames swallowed Natalia, hiding her silhouette in a burst of fire.

— No! Not her! — Robert cried, coming back to himself. Horror distorted his face. — Natalia... I saw her death!

Kenneth rushed to him, supporting his friend. The others exchanged alarmed glances as Robert sat there, clutching his head in his hands. Thoughts of Natalia would not leave him in peace. He did not know whether she was alive after leaving the fortress with the best warriors. Fear and anxiety for the woman he loved tore his heart apart.

— Robert, you have to pull yourself together, — Kenneth said, looking at him anxiously. — Right now the most important thing is to organize a reliable defense of the fortress. Natalia is strong. She will manage.

— According to my calculations, — Sasha said, checking her papers, — the savages’ resources will not last long. If we handle our own supplies wisely, we can hold out.

— And I’ll figure out where to place the traps, — Martin added, the seasoned fighter ready for action. — Those savages will dance before they ever reach our walls.

Kenneth looked thoughtfully into the distance, toward the place beyond the forest where the enemy camp was hidden.

— Perhaps we should try to speak with the savages. Maybe bloodshed can still be avoided.

— It’s dangerous for you to go alone, — Martin objected. — I’ll go with you. The two of us can stand up for ourselves.

Robert raised his tired eyes to them. He understood that negotiation was the last hope for protecting the fortress and waiting for Natalia’s return. They had to take the risk.

— All right. Go, — he said, making the difficult decision. — But be extremely careful. The fate of all of us depends on you.

Kenneth and Martin began preparing for the dangerous sortie, aware that the lives of the fortress residents — and perhaps the fragile chance for peace — were at stake. All of them clung to the hope that Natalia and her warriors were alive and would return soon.

Robert and Sasha stood on the fortress wall, gazing tensely into the distance. Smoke from the campfires of the savages who had besieged the settlement curled on the horizon. The defenders prepared for a long and difficult defense.

Suddenly a man of average height approached them, wearing a beard in the style of Don Quixote. Robert frowned, trying to remember where he might have seen him before. But memory stubbornly refused to help.

— Forgive me, do we know each other? — Robert asked cautiously, hiding his embarrassment. At such a serious moment, he could not allow even a hint of weakness.

The stranger smiled from the corner of his mouth.

— Robert, don’t tell me you don’t recognize me. It’s me, Luis — the architect who designed your fortress.

Luis was a young man of thirty-three, with long hair and a neatly trimmed beard that gave him a special charm and elegance. He always dressed with taste, favoring light shirts and ties that emphasized his refined style. At times he completed his appearance with a bright scarf, carefully knotted around his neck, which made him even more memorable.

Despite the harsh conditions in which they lived, Luis preserved his elegance and confidence, setting him apart from the other inhabitants of the fortress. His way of speaking and carrying himself reflected his past: he had been an architect, and this gave him a special authority. Luis not only understood construction; he also knew how to communicate with people, making him a valuable ally in difficult times.

— Oh, forgive me, Luis, — Robert apologized, rubbing the back of his head in embarrassment. — So much has happened that my head is spinning. Of course I remember that you designed our fortress. It is magnificent.

Luis softened a little. He understood Robert’s condition; things were difficult for everyone now.

— All right, forget it. I didn’t come for praise, — Luis said, glancing toward the forest where the enemies were hidden. — I used to live among those savages. And I know a few things about them.

Robert and Sasha exchanged astonished looks. It sounded unbelievable, because the last thing anyone could say about Luis was that he had once been some kind of savage. His refined style, elegant clothing, and polished manner of speaking were completely at odds with the life of those who hid in the forest. Luis seemed like the embodiment of civilization and culture, and the thought that he had once lived among those people filled them with bewilderment.

— Tell us everything you know, Luis, — Sasha asked. — Any information is worth its weight in gold right now.

Luis sighed and began his account.

— Before the global catastrophe, these people were ordinary citizens. All they cared about was football and politics. They spent hours discussing matches and criticizing those in power, but they never lifted a finger to change anything.

Luis darkened as he remembered the past.

— And they made a fatal mistake: they voted for a leader who promised to end inflation and restore the country’s former glory. He spoke of change, of how everything would become better. But instead, he led the country to a place where its resources were sold off and the people were left with nothing. Many citizens, trusting his promises, found themselves on the edge of survival, suffering from hunger and deprivation. Soon afterward came the catastrophe that swallowed the country, and those who had once believed in a bright future were left only to remember their mistakes, losing all hope of salvation.

Robert clenched his fists. To think that these savages had been the cause of so much suffering.

— Thank you for telling us this, Luis, — Sasha said, placing a hand on his shoulder. — Now we understand better who we are dealing with. We must stand together and protect what we have managed to create.
