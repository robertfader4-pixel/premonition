Robert Fader · Premonition

Chapter 11: A Night Without Comfort

✦

The moon shone brightly in the cloudless sky, bathing the fortress of Our Utopia in a pale silver glow. Only the soft whisper of the wind disturbed the silence of the night. Martin, his heart pounding with agitation, moved carefully through the shadows, determined to escape from the fortress. He knew that what he was about to do was dangerous, but he felt he had no other choice.

As he made his way toward the exit, Sasha noticed him from the window of her room. Without a second thought, she ran after him.

— “Martin!” she called, catching up with him just as he was about to cross the threshold. “Where are you going at this hour?”

Martin stopped and turned toward her. Surprise and nervousness were written across his face.

— “I need to go somewhere secluded to pray,” he answered, trying to sound convincing. “Robert doesn’t like it when we practice religion here.”

Sasha frowned, unconvinced by his explanation.

— “Pray? At this hour? That doesn’t sound right, Martin.”

— “Please, Sasha, trust me. I just need to be alone,” he insisted, trying to calm her.

Sasha felt there was nothing more she could do, and decided to let him go, though her unease did not leave her.

— “All right. But be careful. I don’t want you getting into trouble.”

Martin nodded and walked away, disappearing into the darkness of the night. Sasha remained by the door, sensing that something was wrong, but for the moment she chose to set the incident aside.

The next day, the fortress was alive with activity. Sasha helped prepare the stage for the concert that was to take place in the central square. Excitement hung in the air. Oksana and Kenneth, who had become almost a couple in love, were organizing the final details.

— “I can’t believe how many people have come,” Oksana said, looking around in astonishment. “I never imagined there would be so many people here in Our Utopia.”

Sasha smiled, proud of what they had achieved. No one was drinking alcohol; everyone enjoyed tea or mate, the drink Luis had introduced and which had become popular among the inhabitants of the fortress.

Natalia, the gifted cellist, was also scheduled to perform. Sasha watched her as she tuned her instrument, feeling a mixture of admiration and nervousness.

At last, the concert began. Natalia stepped onto the stage and began to play Premonition. The melody filled the air, and Luis, sitting in the front row, could not contain himself.

— “I love you, Natalia!” he shouted, his voice ringing through the crowd.

Robert, watching from a corner, felt a sharp curiosity at Luis’s excessive reaction. Why was he so desperately in love with Natalia? At that very moment, a violent dizziness seized him. Visions of his wife Natalia began to appear in his mind, and his heart filled with turmoil.

While Natalia played, Robert fell to the ground, but no one noticed because he was far from the crowd. In his mind, the visions grew stronger: a vast army was marching toward Our Utopia, and despair gripped him.

Natalia’s performance came to an end, and Oksana began to sing a song called You Are My Fortress. The applause of the audience was deafening, and joy filled the square. Yet Natalia, sensing that something was wrong, moved toward one of the passages leading away from the plaza.

At that moment, Luis decided to follow her. When he caught up, he saw Robert lying on the ground.

— “What is happening?” Natalia cried, alarmed at the sight of Robert unconscious. “Robert!”

While Oksana continued to sing, the crowd remained enthralled, unaware of the tragedy unfolding nearby. Natalia knelt beside Robert and tried to help him, but he did not respond. In desperation, she kissed him on the lips, hoping it would wake him.

Only then did Robert awaken from his nightmare. His eyes opened abruptly, and the vision of his wife, Natalia, slowly vanished. Confusion and fear still surrounded him, but the warmth of Natalia’s kiss brought him back to reality.

Luis, who had followed the cellist, watched the scene with a broken heart. The connection between them was palpable, and his own declaration of love seemed insignificant compared with the moment they were sharing. He felt like an intruder in a moment that did not belong to him.

Meanwhile, Martin took advantage of the distraction created by the concert to open the gates of the fortress. A group of people, not too large but not small either, began to enter. Among them was Zhenya, who had previously left the fortress because of her disagreements with Robert over economic ideas. Now she seemed to have returned with Martin’s help, and her presence brought with it an atmosphere of uncertainty.

The music continued to play in the square, and the crowd enjoyed the performance, unaware of the events unfolding in the shadows. Martin watched with a satisfied smile as his plan began to take shape. He knew that the arrival of Zhenya and her group could change the balance of power in Our Utopia.

Meanwhile, Natalia tried to bring Robert fully back to consciousness. He was still confused by the visions he had endured. The worry on her face was unmistakable, and her heart beat hard as she saw him in such a state.

— “Robert, please wake up. I need you to be all right,” she said, her voice full of anxiety.

Still fighting through the confusion, Robert finally managed to focus his gaze on Natalia.

— “What happened?” he murmured, trying to remember what he had seen.

— “You were on the ground, and…” Natalia began to explain, but her voice faltered when she saw the fear in Robert’s eyes.

— “I saw an army, Natalia. They are coming here,” he said, his voice trembling.

The music continued in the square, but the atmosphere had changed. The joy of the concert mingled with the growing anxiety of Robert and Natalia. Meanwhile, Martin and his people moved through the shadows, ready to take advantage of any opportunity.

The fate of Our Utopia hung by a thread, and the decisions made in the coming hours could change everything.
