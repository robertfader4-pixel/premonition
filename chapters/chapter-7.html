Robert Fader · Premonition

Chapter 7: Falling Tears

✦

The sun was slowly sinking beyond the horizon when Robert saw the savages advancing toward the fortress. They moved with unusual confidence, and in their hands they carried strange catapults whose very shape seemed threatening. Robert’s heart began to beat faster. He knew that time was running out, and that a decision had to be made.

“Martin! Kenneth!” he shouted, drawing his companions’ attention.

Both men hurried toward him.

“What’s happening?” Martin asked.

“We cannot allow them to fire those catapults,” Robert said. “I am ordering you to use our weapons.”

Martin looked at him with sudden delight.

“Already? Excellent.”

Robert met his gaze without saying a word, but his expression made it clear that there was no other choice. Kenneth, understanding the situation, quickly went toward the northern tower, while Martin headed for the southern one.

At that moment Sasha and Luis approached Robert. Both watched anxiously as their friends prepared to defend Our Utopia.

Kenneth reached his tower and pulled the cover from a weapon Robert had never wanted to use: a rocket launcher. He aimed toward the distant catapults of the savages and pressed the trigger. A blow. One of the catapults flew apart in pieces. For an instant Kenneth felt a surge of triumph, but that feeling soon turned into horror when he saw what happened next.

Martin, having activated his own launcher, did not aim at a catapult. He aimed at the savages themselves. Most of them were wounded, though from such a distance it was impossible to tell whether any had been killed. Martin seemed almost to enjoy the dreadful spectacle.

“Stop!” Robert shouted when he realized what was happening.

The savages seemed to understand that they could no longer continue the assault, and they began to retreat. Against the defensive power of Our Utopia, they had no more chances.

The people inside the fortress began to celebrate, but Robert stopped them.

“This is not a time for celebration,” he said sadly. “For me, this is a very sorrowful day. We failed to achieve peace, and we were forced to use the very weapons we never wanted to use. My idea of Our Utopia was to create a place of peace. Now it has become a target for conquest. The savages who escaped will return sooner or later, and they will try to attack us again. They will pass on what they have learned to someone with the technology capable of reaching our fortress.”

Martin could not resist making an inappropriate joke about the wounded savages, but Robert chose not to respond.

Kenneth suggested going out to help the wounded. Sasha joined him, and together they rode out on a motorcycle toward the place where the savages had fallen, determined to help those who needed care.

Robert remained where he was, lost in troubled thought. He understood that this was only the beginning of a new struggle, and that the world he longed for was still very far from becoming real.

He stood on the observation platform of his fortress, binoculars pressed to his eyes. The sun was already lowering behind the horizon, painting the sky in vivid shades of orange and purple. Yet he was not looking at the beauty of the sunset. His attention was fixed on the savages gathering in groups at a distance. Their confident movements and wary glances filled him with unease. He knew they were not going to withdraw without a fight, and that knowledge promised new troubles.

With every passing moment, Robert felt the tension grow. Our Utopia was not merely a building; it was the place where all of them hoped to build a new world, free from violence and fear. But now, as the savages once again showed aggression, his dream seemed increasingly unreachable. He remembered how they had all worked together to create this refuge, how they had imagined a future in which people could live in peace, no longer afraid of attack or cruelty.

“Did you see how they approached the fortress?” Luis asked, standing beside Sasha. His voice was full of displeasure and concern. “They are not planning to surrender.”

“Yes,” Sasha replied, her face tense with concentration. “But we have to be ready for anything. If they decide to attack again, we will have to repel them.”

Robert listened to them and understood that they were right. The savages were dangerous enemies, and their aggression could end in disaster. He remembered how narrowly they had held back the previous attack, and the memory had left a deep mark on his soul. He did not want to relive it.

Suddenly Martin approached them with a smirk on his face, as though he had just heard something amusing.

“You know, I always said the savages didn’t know how to fight. Look at them. They can’t even hold their weapons properly. If it weren’t for our rockets, they’d all be lying on the ground like swatted flies.”

Robert frowned. He understood that Martin took pleasure in war, and that deeply worried him. It seemed to Robert that Martin was not simply a warrior, but a man who found enjoyment in violence. That was not what their community needed.

“Martin,” Robert said, trying to remain calm, “that is enough. Go and rest. We do not need more violence.”

Martin looked displeased, but he understood that Robert would not change his decision. He nodded and left. Robert watched him go, realizing that Martin was a true fanatic of war, and that this might become a problem in the future. If they could not control their emotions, everything could end in catastrophe.

Meanwhile, Kenneth, Sasha, and Luis decided to go to the wounded savages. They quickly got into the medical van and drove toward the place where the injured lay. When they arrived, a terrible sight met their eyes: dozens of wounded men were scattered across the ground. Some were in critical condition; others groaned in pain. These were the consequences of Martin’s rocket strike.

Kenneth looked at the wounded and thought: What a bastard he is. Instead of frightening them into surrender, Martin had killed them, or nearly killed them. That was not what they had hoped to achieve. Kenneth felt his heart tighten with bitterness. These people, despite their wild way of life, were victims of circumstance, and now they were suffering because of decisions made in anger.

Sasha, organized and attentive by nature, began counting the wounded. She approached one of the savages and asked why they had decided to attack Our Utopia.

“Because it is a beautiful fortress,” he answered, his voice filled with pain. His eyes were full of fear and confusion, as though he could not understand how they had ended up in this situation.

Unable to restrain herself, Sasha kicked him in the chest and pointed out his mistake.

“You could have come here and become part of this place. You could have helped us build it. You could have left your savagery behind. Instead, you chose war — just like those ‘civilized savages’ who destroyed our world with nuclear fire.”

Her words were full of anger and disappointment. She understood that their struggle was not only against a physical enemy, not only against ideas, but against the thoughts that had brought this conflict into being. Robert, observing her reaction, felt his own emotions grow hot. He knew they had to find a way past hatred, or they would never build the world they dreamed of.

“Sasha,” he said, trying to calm her, “let us try to understand why they acted this way. Perhaps they have reasons of their own. If we can establish a dialogue, perhaps we can avoid further conflicts.”

Kenneth, standing nearby, nodded.

“Maybe they believe they are defending their land. We have to understand their point of view before judging them.”

Sasha, still angry, stared at them.

“How can we trust them? They already attacked us.”

“We cannot continue living in fear,” Robert replied. “If we act only out of fear, we will become the same as them. We must be better.”

Luis, who had remained silent until then, finally spoke.

“I agree with Robert. If we want to build a new world, we must learn to forgive and to understand. Otherwise, we will never escape this cycle of violence.”

Sasha sighed, realizing that her anger would lead nowhere good.

“All right. I will try. But it will not be easy.”

Robert nodded, feeling that they were moving in the right direction.

“Let us talk to them. Perhaps we can find a common language.”

They returned to the wounded savages, and Robert, gathering his courage, began to speak.

“We do not want war. We want peace. Why did you decide to attack us?”

The savage whom Sasha had kicked looked at him in confusion.

“We did not want to attack. We wanted to protect our lands. You came onto our territory and built your fortress here.”

Robert felt his heart tighten.

“We never meant to harm anyone. We were only searching for a safe place to live. We want everyone to be able to live in peace.”

The savage nodded, pain still written on his face.

“We want peace too. But we do not know how to reach it. We have lost many people, and now we fear for our lives.”

Sasha felt her anger begin to fade.

“We can help you. If you agree to a truce, we can work together and find a solution.”

The savage looked at her with hope.

“You truly want to help us?”

“Yes,” Sasha answered, and her voice softened. “We all want to live in peace. Let us try to find a way.”

Robert, seeing the dialogue begin to take shape, felt a surge of hope. Perhaps they really could change the situation. Perhaps they could build the world they had imagined.

Martin, who had been watching from a distance, felt his anger fade. He could not believe his friends were trying to reach an understanding with their enemies. It was not his way. Yet as he looked at the suffering he himself had caused, he began to understand that his approach to war had been wrong.

“I have listened patiently, but I cannot stay silent anymore,” he said, approaching them. “I was blind. Now I see that violence solves nothing. I thought I could control the situation, but all I did was make it worse. I do not want to be part of this cycle of hatred anymore.”

Robert, surprised by the change in Martin, looked at him with hope.

“That matters, Martin. We can all make mistakes. What matters is the desire to change. If you are ready to work on yourself, that is already a step in the right direction.”

Martin nodded, his face serious.

“I want to help. I do not know how, but I am ready to learn. I do not want to see any more suffering caused by my own hands.”

Sasha, sensing that the atmosphere had begun to change, turned to the wounded savages.

“We can create a safe space for everyone. If you agree to a truce, we can work together to restore what has been destroyed.”

The savage who had spoken with Robert looked at his people. He understood that this might be their only chance for peace.

“We agree. We want to try. But we need time to rebuild trust.”

Robert smiled as he felt hope begin to take root.

“That will be the first step. Let us begin with small things. We can organize a meeting where everyone can express what they think and feel. It will help us understand one another better.”

Kenneth added:

“We can create joint groups to work on common projects. That will help us build trust.”

“Yes,” Sasha said. “And we can begin by helping the wounded. That is a good way to show that we are ready to work together.”

The savage smiled, though pain still marked his face.

“We agree. This may be the beginning of something new.”

Robert looked at his friends as the tension began to ease.

“Then let us do it. We can change the course of events if we work together.”

They began discussing the details. Each of them knew that many difficulties lay ahead, but they were ready for them. The path to peace would be long and difficult, yet it was the only path they could choose.

Time passed, and little by little relations began to improve. The savages and the inhabitants of Our Utopia worked together, restoring damaged areas and helping one another. Every step brought them closer to understanding and acceptance.

Martin, once filled with hatred, now became one of those who actively participated in the restoration. He realized that his former views had been mistaken, and now he searched for ways to help others. His transformation inspired many, and soon he became a symbol of change.

Sasha, watching everything shift around her, felt her anger recede. She understood that hatred could not be the answer to their problems. Instead, she began searching for ways to help others and to build new relationships.

Robert, seeing their efforts bear fruit, felt they were on the right path. Much work still lay ahead, but now they had hope — hope that they could build the world they had dreamed of.

In the end, they organized a great celebration to mark what they had achieved. It was a time when everyone could gather, leave the past behind, and begin a new life. Music, laughter, and joy filled the air, and each person felt part of something greater.

Standing among his friends and new allies, Robert understood that they had made the right choice. They had chosen the path of understanding and cooperation, and that was the only way to build a world free from violence and fear.
