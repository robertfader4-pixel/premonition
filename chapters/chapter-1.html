Robert Fader · Premonition

Chapter 1: Passions

✦

Robert rode slowly through the ruined streets on his motorcycle, making his way through the debris of civilization. He was a man of average height, thirty-eight years old, with a short, untidy beard and a neatly trimmed mustache that gave his face a somewhat stern appearance, though there was still something kind in it. His motorcycle, an old machine from the twentieth century, produced a distinctive growl that echoed through the empty streets. With every turn, he became more aware of how deeply the world had changed. Once, these buildings had embodied hope and progress. Now they stood only as witnesses to the collapse of humanity’s highest aspirations. The world had changed, and people had vanished from it.

As he looked at the abandoned structures, he realized that his memories of the past belonged to a life now impossibly distant. He was one of the few who had survived the apocalypse. The bunker he had built in advance had become his only salvation. Now he wandered among the ruins, dreaming of creating a new life and a new civilization. He was searching for those who shared his vision — people willing to build a fortress-city, a place he would call Utopia.

When Robert noticed a small dugout in the distance, his heart began to beat faster. Two people lived there — a man and a woman. Dima was short and slight, with delicate features and large, clear eyes behind his glasses. He looked tired, yet there was still hope in his gaze. Masha, a little taller than Dima, had long hair framing her face. She seemed calm and relaxed, but whenever anger took hold of her, her expression became strict and unyielding. They had not seen another living soul for two years. Filled with sudden enthusiasm, Robert stopped his motorcycle, overcome by the feeling that he had finally found the people he had been looking for.

He approached them, introduced himself, and quickly explained his idea.

— “I want to create Utopia,” he said. “A place where people can once again find safety and peace. We can build a new city based on trust and mutual support.”

Dima studied Robert with obvious interest, already sinking into thoughts about the proposal. His face lit up, as though he could already see the possibility of a new and fascinating path.

Masha, however, remained wary. She had seen too many utopias born from deception and cruelty. Her anxiety came from the knowledge that the world around them was full of dangers, and trusting anyone had become too great a risk.

— “But we have already survived here, in this place,” she said, trying to understand his motives. “Maybe we should stay where we are.”

There was determination in her voice, the kind that belongs to someone who has endured far more than appearances suggest.

Robert thought of Natalia, and sadness filled his heart. She had been a woman with fiery red hair that seemed to glow in the sunlight, and gray-green eyes that reflected the full depth of her soul. Her nose was thin and slightly pointed, giving her face an elegant sharpness, while her confident, penetrating gaze had always filled Robert with admiration. Natalia knew how to find beauty in simple things, and her desire to help others was not merely work — it was a calling. Every time she smiled, the world around her seemed brighter. But now, without her beside him, Robert felt that brightness fading. Her determination and independence had filled him with pride, yet they had also caused him pain, because he understood that he had not been able to keep her with him. Now she was one of the many who might have disappeared from his life forever, leaving behind only the memory of how she had once illuminated his days.

Standing before Masha and Dima, he felt those memories of Natalia pierce through him — her laughter, her confident steps as she hurried to help someone in need. Every moment spent without her seemed to carry him farther away from the world they had once shared. He understood that, despite all his dreams, the images of the past would not release him. The hope he wanted to give others was itself in need of rescue.

— “I understand your fears,” he said, trying to reach Masha. “But together we can create something better. We will have opportunities we never had before. I have seen bunkers. I know how to survive and how to build.”

He spoke with fire in his eyes, desperate to convince them both that he was not merely a dreamer, but a man with a concrete plan.

Dima, still intrigued, encouraged Masha.

— “Maybe we should try,” he said. “We won’t find anyone else in this post-apocalyptic world…”

But his words met Masha’s cold stare, full of distrust and doubt.

Meanwhile, Robert felt a conflict rising within him: the light he longed to restore to the world was struggling against the shadow left by Natalia’s loss. He did not know what his next step would be. And although he dreamed of a new life, his heart remained torn, caught on a past that had once seemed so essential.

Masha and Dima, who at first had seemed to Robert like an embodiment of calm in this ruined world, suddenly began to argue. The passions they had concealed now burst into the open.

— “What are you getting so worked up about?” Masha shouted. Her voice rolled like thunder. “This man could destroy us. We survived here, and now you’re ready to take an unjustified risk for the sake of some dreams?”

— “Have you lost your mind?” Dima replied, his eyes flashing with anger. “We have a chance at a better future. We can’t sit here waiting for someone to find us, or for us to die of hunger. This is our chance.”

The atmosphere grew heated, as if inside a forge. Standing between them, Robert felt his own emotions seeking release through the pain of loss. He watched old grievances and fears flare up, watched words full of passion and resentment fly in every direction.

Masha, no longer holding back her tears, continued:

— “Maybe you’re ready to believe in a happy ending, but I have seen how quickly this world can take away everything we have. I am not going to risk my life for a dream built on the unknown.”

Dima did not stop.

— “And what do you suggest? Living in fear? Hiding in a hole and waiting until bandits find us, or hunger takes us? You don’t understand. We have to move forward. We can’t remain trapped in one place. We have to search for hope.”

Robert looked from one to the other and remembered his Natalia. He remembered how they had once discussed their dreams, how passionately she had spoken about her love for life and how important it was not to be afraid of change. Every time she laughed and challenged him, he felt his soul fill with energy. Now, listening to this argument, fragments of those bright memories became unbearably bitter.

— “Do you remember how we dreamed about the future?” Dima said to Masha. “We talked about our hopes, about where we wanted to live, about how we wanted to help others. I’m not proposing a utopia that can’t be built. I’m proposing a possibility, a chance at a new life.”

Masha suddenly fell silent. Her anger softened, and in her eyes there appeared again a shadow of sorrow and memory. She remembered sitting with Dima by the fire, sharing hopes and dreams. She remembered how every moment of their life had been filled with the expectation of good changes — changes that had never come.

— “Perhaps you’re right,” she said quietly. Her voice had lost some of its sharpness. “But can we really trust someone who appeared out of nowhere and is asking us to leave our home?”

Dima, noticing the shift in her tone, tried to support her.

— “You know that doing everything the old way isn’t a solution. We can hope, Masha, but we also have to act. Let Robert give us a chance. Let there be a chance.”

At that moment, Robert once again felt waves of emotion roll over him. He remembered the days when Natalia came home tired, but proud. He remembered how they had discussed difficult decisions together, and how their hearts had filled with the desire to pursue a common purpose. Every conversation with her had been full of passion; every argument had been more than words. It had been life itself.

Robert’s gaze passed over Masha and Dima’s faces, both filled with contradiction. He remembered that this feeling — passion — could give birth to love as easily as hatred. These emotions, rising from the depths of the heart, surrounded them like a whirlwind, forcing them to make choices they had not even realized were before them.

— “I am not asking you to stop doubting,” he said carefully. “I am only offering a possibility. Perhaps fear is natural. But dreams also have a right to exist. We have a chance to create something better.”

Robert’s words hung in the air. And although his voice sounded calm, feelings churned inside him. He knew that each of them had to make a choice. Like him, each carried within themselves a pillar of passion. And however difficult it was to move forward, he understood that in this new world, passions could become both a destructive and a creative force.

Masha suddenly turned to Robert, her eyes shining with determination. The tension and agitation had reached their limit, and she could no longer allow the situation to continue.

— “Enough,” she said, her voice firm. “I will not risk my life for empty words and promises. You do not understand what we have been through. We look after ourselves. We survive. And we have survived because we have always been careful with strangers.”

Dima, whose heart was divided, tried to find words that might reconcile them. But Masha interrupted him.

— “No, Dima,” she exclaimed. “You need to understand that trusting a stranger is madness. He may be just like the people who led the world to ruin. All these passions, this struggle for power, this fear and hatred — that is why we ended up here, in this hell.”

Robert felt his hopes melt like ice beneath a scorching sun. He looked at them both, and chaos raged in his soul. The memory of Natalia — her passion for life, her devotion to medicine, her desire to help — suddenly reminded him that their own passions had also been the very thing that destroyed the world.

— “But how do we rid ourselves of passions?” he said softly, as though the question was addressed not only to them, but to himself. “Were these not the very emotions that led to war and destruction? How can we escape them when they live inside us?”

Hearing this, Masha softened a little.

— “We cannot rid ourselves of passions, Robert. We can only learn to control them. Human feelings are what make us human. But they are also what cause conflicts. If we allow fear and ambition to rule us, we will never build the world you dream of.”

The image of Natalia rose before him again. With tenderness, Robert remembered how they had sometimes argued about what it meant to be human. She had always believed that true humanity was rooted precisely in passions — in the desire to help, to care, to reach out to others. Yet she also knew, not from hearsay, how easily fear and hatred could turn into something destructive.

— “I am not going to abandon you,” he said, unable to hide his desperation. “I can’t. I believe we can create something remarkable.”

— “But that is not your decision,” Masha replied firmly, throwing her hands forward as if defending herself. “If you need a new home, I can tell you plainly: it is not here. Leave.”

Robert felt his heart tighten with pain. He understood that he had lost the chance to build Utopia with them, but he also felt more clearly than ever that the world he longed for was full of contradictions. They were right: the human passions that had ignited in the past had become the sparks that fed the flames of nuclear catastrophe. If people did not learn to control them, any new society would face the same problems again.

— “I am leaving,” he said, his voice quiet. “But remember this: the world needs more than survival. The world needs hope.”

Dima did not know what to say. His heart was pierced by confusion. He watched as Robert mounted his motorcycle, and the engine’s roar broke the heavy silence.

When Robert left, Masha and Dima remained standing where they were, filled with tension and uncertainty. Their home was an empty place that had once been filled with fear, and now with doubt as well.

In the air lingered the quiet horror of realization: passions were not merely a private matter. They were deeply connected to the catastrophes that had happened in the world. And no matter how hard people tried to hide their emotions, sooner or later those emotions would turn against them.

— “What are we going to do now?” Dima asked, bitterness and confusion in his voice.

— “We will live,” Masha said with difficulty, as though trying to convince herself more than him. “But we must remember that passion is a fragile flower, and it must be protected. We cannot allow it to destroy us.”

And in this lay the contradiction: the fate of each of them could be shaped by those human emotions that were both so precious and so destructive.
