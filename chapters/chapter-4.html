Robert Fader · Premonition

Chapter 4: The Intriguer

✦

Two years had already passed since Alexey had left this world, leaving Robert alone with his dreams and hopes. In that time, many noble souls had answered the call to build a new society. At the heart of that undertaking stood the fortress they had named Our Utopia. The name became a symbol of resistance against all those who had ever doubted the possibility of building a better world. More than once, Robert remembered how his dreams, filled with light and hope, had been dismissed by most people as an empty ideology — a utopia. One conversation with his mother remained especially vivid in his memory: one day, with bitterness in her voice, she had declared that the world could not be changed. She had not even been able to explain what a utopia was, and Robert, offended by her pessimism, had merely smiled and replied: It exists — and I will build it.

The fort known as Our Utopia rose majestically on a hill, dominating the surrounding land. Its pentagonal structure, painted a brilliant turquoise, glittered in the sunlight and formed a striking contrast with the greenery around it. The color symbolized not only hope and renewal; it also made the fortress visible from afar, turning it into a beacon for anyone seeking refuge.

The fortress had been designed with two rings of defense, each strengthened by solid walls that provided an additional layer of protection. The first ring, closer to the outside, was equipped with watchtowers that rose toward the sky, allowing the guards to observe every movement on the horizon. These towers, cylindrical in shape and pierced by narrow windows, were decorated with ornaments reflecting the culture and ideals of the community living within.

At the center of the fortress stood the main tower, proudly rising beside the central square. Taller and more slender than the others, it served as the heart of Our Utopia. From its summit one could see the entire valley and the lands beyond, making it strategically important both for defense and for planning. The central square, located directly beside the tower, was a lively meeting place where residents gathered to exchange ideas, celebrate holidays, and strengthen the bonds of the community.

Access to the fortress was not easy. The hill on which it stood was steep and abrupt, making it difficult for vehicles to approach. That gave the settlement a natural layer of security, since only those who truly wished to join the community, or who came with a clear purpose, could reach its gates. The roads leading to the fortress were framed by trees and vegetation, creating an atmosphere of peace and isolation from the outside world.

In every sense, Our Utopia was not merely a physical fortress, but a symbol of resistance and hope — a refuge where the ideals of its inhabitants could flourish in safety and protection.

In Our Utopia, Robert sought to create a society free from fear and greed, a place where every person could feel that they mattered. Each day, as he walked through the fortress surrounded by people who believed in his dream, he understood that he had begun not simply a task, but the construction of a future.

Yet soon discord entered his idealistic society through Zhenya. She had been among those who came to the fortress almost from the beginning, and for a time she enjoyed Robert’s trust.

She was a brunette with short hair that neatly framed her face. She had an expressive, prominent nose and a wide mouth, giving her appearance a distinctive character. Her rather sharp features created a unique and memorable image, one that drew attention and curiosity. In her eyes one could see intelligence and determination. Zhenya possessed a charisma that helped her win the confidence of those around her.

She spoke with assurance about the knowledge she had acquired before the catastrophe and wanted to use it to improve life in the fortress, which made her an important figure in Robert’s idealistic society. She insisted that what she had known before the collapse could help them during this new stage. One day, after making up her mind, she approached Robert and said:

— There is too much chaos in the fortress. We need a currency in order to organize labor and resources. It will help us establish an economy and prevent the waste of human effort.

Robert was stunned. Why would they need that, he wondered, when everyone was already working together? Their untainted society rested on trust and mutual assistance, not on greed.

— Zhenya, — he answered, trying to remain calm, — we are doing perfectly well without money. Our economy does not need coins. It was money and greed that helped bring the world to catastrophe.

But Zhenya had no intention of giving up. She insisted:

— I have a plan that will work. The currency will be backed by each person’s labor, and that way we will be able to control resources and increase people’s responsibility.

Her words struck Robert painfully. He did not want to divide their society. The idea of creating currency seemed to open the door to the individualism he had worked so carefully to restrain. Questions of personal gain began to rise in his mind, and grains of doubt slowly crept into his soul.

Two days later, rumors began to spread through the fortress that Robert was supposedly ineffective as a leader. He noticed certain people exchanging glances in passing and sensed discontent growing in the air. At that moment he understood that intrigues were being woven behind his back — and that Zhenya was their driving force.

While Robert tried to preserve peace in Our Utopia, open arguments began between his supporters and Zhenya’s. They debated how life in the fortress should be organized, and more and more people, yielding to the magic of her words, began to cast a shadow over Robert. In the end, roughly a quarter of the population of Our Utopia decided to leave and build a utopia of their own. This rare but significant shift struck his heart like a hammer: how difficult it was to create a new world when people so quickly became victims of their own ambitions.

Sleep would not come that night. Robert sat on the edge of his bed, immersed in a whirlpool of thought. He understood that the circumstances in which he found himself were incredibly complex. What had once seemed bright and beautiful now threatened to dissolve into chaos. His heart tightened at the thought of how to win back the trust of the people for whom he had fought so long — and how not to lose sight of the main purpose.

Rain drummed against the roofs of Our Utopia, plunging the fortress into a mysterious half-darkness. Suddenly there came a quiet knock at the door. Robert, gazing thoughtfully through the window into the gloomy light, turned and saw Kenneth standing in the doorway. He was soaked to the skin. His face was full of worry.

Kenneth was a tall, sturdy young man of about thirty. His powerful build was almost cabinet-like, and he radiated confidence and strength. His dark chestnut hair was slightly disheveled, giving him a look that was careless and masculine at once. Despite his imposing size, there was kindness and determination in his eyes. It was clear that he came from the Great Power, though his life had nothing in common with its past: he had fled from it long before the catastrophe, leaving behind all the horrors and ambitions that had once surrounded him. Now he had become part of Our Utopia, striving to build a new society free from fear and oppression.

— Robert, we have a problem, — he said in a trembling voice. — They say the savages have learned about us.

Kenneth had become Robert’s right hand in this difficult journey. A Yankee, originally from a part of the country known for its traditions and reliability, he had ended up in the desert, where his life depended on the invisible thread of fate. A year earlier, Robert had found him exhausted and on the edge of death, wandering through desolate expanses where only rage and heat waited for unfortunate travelers.

Kenneth was an experienced tracker, skilled in navigation and survival — knowledge that had become vital to Our Utopia. His almost mystical ability to recognize threats and opportunities in harsh conditions had inspired many members of the fortress. When he learned of Robert’s plans to create a protected place for people who still hoped for a better future, he understood that he had found an ally in the struggle for a new world.

And now, with news that the savages had discovered Our Utopia, Kenneth became the one who brought Robert back to reality.

— They may be dangerous, Robert. We have to prepare, — he insisted, his eyes flashing with determination. — We must reinforce the wall, organize patrols, and perhaps form an alliance with those who are close to us.

Robert nodded, grasping the seriousness of the situation. He knew that many of his plans for building a new society might now be under threat. Yet looking at Kenneth, he felt a measure of calm. Fortunately, he was not alone.

In the post-apocalyptic world there existed groups of people, mostly men, who lived as nomads. After the great catastrophe, many of them had joined together into a large wandering community that arose in the south of the country, an area spared by the nuclear blast. Yet that region had been devastated, for its resources had been exhausted even before the catastrophe.

These people, known as the Savages, were not truly savages; their behavior was dictated by the necessity of survival amid chaos. They raided, stole, and attacked, claiming whatever they could find. In a world where no state remained to regulate life among the ruins, they used anarchy to their advantage, becoming a threat to anyone trying to restore society and build a new life.

— Let’s gather everyone, — Robert said decisively, lifting his chin. — We need to discuss this. We still have a chance to change the situation before it gets out of control. We must act quickly, — he continued, collecting his thoughts. — Each of us has to understand that what is at stake is not only our society, but the lives of the people who believe in it.

Kenneth nodded, his face set with resolve.

— I’ll bring everyone who can come. We have to be ready for anything.

Robert rose, his heart beating faster. He felt the tension growing, but at the same time a sense of responsibility was rising within him. He knew that now, more than ever, he had to be the leader he had always wanted to become.

Kenneth left, and Robert remained alone with his thoughts. He looked out the window, where the rain continued to fall, as if nature itself could feel his anxiety. At that moment he understood that the struggle for Our Utopia was only beginning — and that he could not allow himself to surrender.
